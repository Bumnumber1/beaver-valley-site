What is Beaver Valley?

A town that talks back

Beaver Valley is a Unity 2D CRPG / beat-em-up hybrid — “without the inconsequential beating” — set in a smog-choked town whose refinery is named FOUNDRYSTOP. On the surface it’s a side-scroller with brutal ragdoll physics, limb destruction, real blocking, and an arsenal of gleefully stupid weapons. Underneath, it’s a living town.

The hook is the cast. Over 430 named characters fill the Valley. They pursue their own goals, hold conversations, react to what you do to them by name, and can fight you, flee, join your party, or call the cops. Eligible speakers answer in their assigned voices.

It’s one persistent world where consequences stick. A fight can carry through a doorway, a witness can turn it into a police case, and damaged locations can stay damaged.

Why it’s different

Seven reasons it shouldn’t work — and does

01

Your character already has a life

Start as a musician, a police officer, or a sailor. Who you choose determines where you begin and which duties are already waiting for you.

02

You talk. The town answers.

Address someone through your microphone. Characters who can speak answer in their own voices and can draw on what just happened around them.

03

Where you land the hit matters

A block can stop a blow. A hit can injure an arm and change what a fighter can hold, or injure a leg and change how they move.

04

People have somewhere to be

Residents walk to work, meet up at public venues, and head home through the same doors you use. Someone you’re looking for can leave before you get there.

05

The street fight can reach a courtroom

A witness can report what they saw to an officer. An arrest can carry the incident into a trial with testimony, a verdict, and sentencing.

06

Perform a set or investigate a murder

Take the drum part with the whole band on stage, or work a murder case by questioning guests and keeping track of clues in your notebook.

07

The bridge is a room you can walk into

Board a ship and move between its stations. Take the helm, give fleet orders from the chart, or switch to a carrier aircraft when the operation allows it.

A hundred ways to cause a problem

Endless things to do

This is a sandbox, not a corridor. A partial list of what a single evening in the Valley can turn into:

  • Hold court — as judge, defendant, or lawyer
  • Run a naval operation from one of several stations
  • Go to war with a 4-player squad
  • Host a drunk telethon for your Twitch chat
  • Rob the bank — or take out a loan you can’t repay
  • Get arrested and stand trial
  • Break out of jail
  • Serve your sentence on a working cell block
  • Start a bar fight
  • Take a part in Beaver Machine Live on vocals, guitar, bass or drums
  • Sit in on a TV taping
  • Earn all nine martial-arts belts
  • Enter a tournament
  • Recruit a party of AI souls
  • Hand someone a weapon and see what they do
  • Exorcise a ghost
  • Raise the dead (results may vary)
  • Visit the Beaver Dig Site to explore the buried valley
  • A time machine?
  • Open the Murder House notebook to organize the evidence
  • Investigate a haunting with equipment from GhostCrushers
  • Play the stock market
  • Get patched up at the hospital — then dodge the bill
  • Talk directly to a resident using the microphone control
  • Take a submarine to sea — then bring it home into the pen
  • Shoot down a missile before it reaches your hull
  • Con a frigate by voice — and have the crew answer you
  • Fight a flood compartment by compartment
  • Go looking in the trench for whatever took the last boat
  • Fill a fishing hold and sell it on the quay
  • Set up an exercise in the Ocean Operations mission editor
  • Rest at home and pass time
  • Deliver a sealed parcel for the Post Office
  • Buy a boat at the marina
  • …or anything in between

Most of that will kill you the first time. There is a guide to why.

It’s also a variety show

Live music & a crossover cast

Play a four-lane chart from the songbook, or watch the full band from the bar. Beaver Machine Live supports single songs, full sets and imported tracks.

The Valley’s crossover cast draws from Dumpster Talk, Homeless in Denton, Put It In Your Mouth, Namasensei’s, McDoogles and The Smashed Update.
