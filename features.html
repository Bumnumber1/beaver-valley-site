What’s Built

A closer look at the working systems, venues, modes and naval operations in the current game.

01 / The Cast

The Cast

People to meet, recruit, and play.

Beaver Valley’s cast includes residents, service workers, naval crews and characters who appear through specific encounters. Select All in Story character select to browse the playable roster.

  • Conversations draw on a character’s identity and recent events.
  • The game mixes generated responses, recorded scenes and silent roles.
  • The two repo agents appear through debt collection.
02 / Simulation

A Living Town That Runs Itself

Follow the residents from work to the bar and back home.

Story Mode runs a two-hour timetable. Eligible residents walk to their stops through authored doors and divide their time between duties, public outings and home.

  • A starting post can differ from a permanent home.
  • A recruited party member follows the party’s orders ahead of the normal itinerary.
  • Private rooms, active incidents and special encounters have their own entry rules.
03 / Agency

Souls That Act

Watch decisions play out on the street.

Residents use the game’s real movement, inventory and encounter systems. Depending on the character and context, they can speak, fight, flee, assist or react to a witnessed incident.

  • Hold Talk and direct your speech toward the person you mean.
  • A named witness and an officer can become part of the same case.
  • Recruitment changes an eligible character’s current assignment.
04 / Voices

The Voices of Beaver Valley

Generated conversation shares the town with recorded performances.

Characters with speech enabled can respond aloud in their assigned voice. Authored scenes and recorded videos provide other parts of the story, while some characters, including the Zombie Chefs, stay silent.

  • Speech can appear in a bubble while the audio plays.
  • Relevant memories and nearby events can feed into a response.
  • Telethon and theatre material have their own presentation rules.
05 / Music

Live Bands & Real Music

Play vocals, guitar, bass or drums.

Beaver Machine Live runs from the main menu or the band’s song desk. Select a part and play its four-lane chart with taps, holds and a combo multiplier. In the bar, the full band plays beside the note highway.

  • Jeremy plays drums, Andrew bass, Aki guitar and Max vocals.
  • Choose a single song or a full set.
  • The songbook supports importing your own music.
06 / Crossover

A Crossover Cast & the Twitch Telethon

See familiar faces on set and around town.

The cast draws from Dumpster Talk, Homeless in Denton, Put It In Your Mouth, McDoogles and The Smashed Update. Telethon runs its own live broadcast from the main menu and supports Twitch integration when configured.

  • The broadcast has its own participant and caller events.
  • Residents from the shows can also appear in Story Mode.
  • The theatre screens recorded performances.
07 / Justice

The Courtroom

Try a case from the menu or follow one after a Story arrest.

Trial play follows a court sequence with testimony, argument, deliberation, a verdict and sentencing where applicable. The selected cast and case determine who appears.

  • Court Mode can be started independently.
  • Listen for whose turn it is before speaking.
  • The guide covers the path from custody to trial.
08 / Justice

Police, Crime & Doing Time

An incident can follow you from the street into custody.

Police respond to observed or reported incidents and can pursue eligible suspects, including through normal doorway travel. The station opens with Jesse Ventura as chief and Biden on intake.

  • Other officers start on patrol, at home or with the police helicopter.
  • Municipal jail and federal detention have separate procedures.
  • Custody and release follow the current case and sentence.
09 / Economy

The Beaver Valley Economy

A wallet, a bank account and an overdue bill.

Beaver Valley Bank offers savings, loans, and a stock screen that displays prices as well as the player’s wallet and savings. Stores, casino games and hospital services each have their own prices.

  • Outstanding debt can trigger a repossession encounter.
  • Your home stash stores real items across visits and saves.
  • Visit the marina to compare and buy small watercraft.
10 / Modes

Whole Games Within the Game

Choose a courtroom, battlefield, arena, band or fleet.

The main menu includes Story, Versus, Court, War, Telethon, Poker, Beaver Slap (447 cards), Beaver Machine Live, Murder Mystery and Ocean Operations. Other activities, including the dojo, belong to venues inside Story.

  • the Murder House is a playable murder case with a notebook.
  • Poker runs a game of Texas hold’em.
  • Ocean Operations starts with a mission briefing and vessel selection.
11 / World

A Hand-Built World

Town streets lead to shops, venues, sewers and working ports.

The map connects furnished homes, businesses, underground routes, public buildings and ship interiors. Different doors serve different areas, and some facilities require access or mission progress.

  • Housing and duty postings are separate.
  • A player can use a home stash or rest to recover and pass time.
  • Explore locations using the World atlas.
12 / Characters

The Roster & Customizer

Pick a character or create one.

Story and Versus have separate selection flows with category tabs. Character creation provides tools for selecting or editing a character’s appearance.

  • Use All for the complete eligible Story roster.
  • Naval Officers includes enlisted sailors.
  • Enemies such as the male lion belong to encounters.
13 / Combat

Physics Combat & Reactive AI

Feet, reach, and contact determine whether a swing lands.

Weapons and body parts collide in the game world. A successful block can stop the swing before it reaches a vulnerable limb, and an opponent standing at the wrong depth can be outside your reach.

  • Watch individual limb health and what the opponent is holding.
  • Story injuries and Versus bouts follow different persistence rules.
  • Physical doors remain available during combat when other access rules are met.
14 / Weapons

The Arsenal

Carry ammunition, clothing and tools alongside weapons.

Shops and character loadouts include guns, melee weapons, ammo and utility items. Guns use real projectiles, and the selected ammunition can change the effect of a shot.

  • Hands, worn equipment and inventory have separate slots.
  • The gun shop is a place to browse weapons.
  • Weapon handling depends on the equipped item and the condition of the fighter.
15 / Carnage

Gore, Fire, Liquids & Status Effects

Injuries and hazards affect the part of the body they reach.

Individual parts take damage and injuries can change what you can hold or how you move. Environmental hazards and status effects can carry consequences into the next encounter.

  • Use the hospital for treatment appropriate to your injuries.
  • Fire and persistent destruction have dedicated rules.
  • A knocked-out or wounded fighter has different actions from a healthy one.
16 / Companions

Party & Companions

Bring people with you and direct them in the field.

Eligible residents can join the party through native interactions. Party membership takes precedence over routine travel, and control can change when a character is injured, detained or travelling through a doorway.

  • Approach the person you want to recruit.
  • Use your current party roster to see who is with you.
  • Named campaign and ship duties can restrict which actors are available.
17 / Multiplayer

Couch Co-op, Up to Four

Share a local session with friends.

Supported local modes allow up to four human players. Character selection and controls belong to each player slot, and the available number of seats depends on the activity.

  • Story supports a local party of up to four.
  • War supports local squad play.
  • In Versus, fighter slots can be assigned to people or AI.
18 / Medicine

The Hospital & Emergency Services

Treatment depends on what actually happened to you.

The hospital has reception, staff and treatment options for wounds, ailments and missing limbs. Its menu lists the current prices.

  • Basic wound treatment is listed at $30.
  • Cutty and Nurse Green have hospital duty posts.
  • Treatment and physical recovery depend on the injury or ailment.
19 / Voice

Talk to the Town Out Loud

Direct your speech to a person or use a supported command station.

Hold the speech control, choose the intended recipient, and speak into your microphone. Local Story conversations and shipboard orders have different controls and eligibility.

  • Keep the recipient nearby and available.
  • Speech recognition can interpret your words and show the result on screen.
  • Story and naval voice controls are covered in the guide.
20 / Nightlife

The Club & Synchronized Dance

Visit the nightlife venues and catch a performance.

UNSE is a club on the strip. Music and dance also appear in authored events, including the graveyard performance tied to the Kitchen Crypt storyline.

  • Different venues have their own music and crowd behavior.
  • Beaver Machine Live lets you take a separate playable role.
  • The bar and club are separate places.
21 / Vices

Booze, Drugs & Bad Decisions

Drinks and other consumables carry consequences.

Bars and shops sell real inventory items. Drinking and other status effects can alter movement, aim and behavior. Check the character’s condition before starting a fight or trying to travel.

  • Consumables come from the bag or a store.
  • Try the treatment appropriate to the current ailment.
  • A bar visit can also lead to music or an encounter.
22 / Destruction

Wreck It and It Stays Wrecked

Some structures carry the damage forward.

Supported scenes use destruction changes that persist in the current world. The visuals and gameplay consequences depend on the target and the event that damaged it.

  • Grand FOB has authored destruction presentations.
  • Damaged vessels have separate compartment and system rules.
  • A preview, versus match or separate operation uses its own state.
23 / Story

Scripted Scenes, Unscripted Souls

Authored scenes set the stage. Story systems shape the response.

Beaver Valley mixes authored performances and encounters with characters that react to the current world. Saved events, native travel and on-screen conversations each have their own place in the story.

  • The theatre includes recorded material.
  • the Murder House uses a case-specific cast and evidence.
  • The Kitchen Crypt ritual has requirements and a later graveyard event.
24 / The House

The Casino & the Family

Spend your own cash at the tables.

The casino runs blackjack, roulette, slots and poker. Check the table and your wallet before playing. The poker room also supports a separate Texas hold’em session from the menu.

  • Don Alder’s crew works the casino floor.
  • The poker interface shows your cards and the pot.
  • Cash used at a Story table affects the player’s wallet.
25 / The Arcade

James’s Barcade

Playable cabinets beside the bar.

Visit James’s Barcade and approach a cabinet with the entry price in your wallet. Ancient Dam pinball is one of the games you can play there.

  • The cabinet opens its own controls.
  • The playfield shows score and the current ball.
  • The bar and cabinets share the venue.
26 / Campaign

The Grand FOB

Rest, resupply, and pick the next assignment between deployments.

Intelligence tracks campaign assignments and territory. The barracks advances time and treats surviving limbs, and the communications room schedules support for the chosen mission.

  • Use the duty bunk to advance one day and recover up to 25% maximum HP per surviving limb.
  • Command orders a mission after two off-duty days.
  • The first support package for the mission is free. Further packages cost $500 for artillery, $750 for precision strikes, or $950 for heavy bombing.
27 / Annihilation

Buildings Can Be Destroyed

Return to see the damage an attack leaves behind.

Strike damage can change supported locations in the active world. At McDoogles the damaged site remains visible after the impact.

  • McDoogles has its own destruction aftermath.
  • Durability and damage are tracked separately for ships.
  • A location’s available scenes depend on its authored state.
28 / Culture

The Theatre

Buy a ticket, take a seat, or operate the lighting board.

The Beaver Valley Theatre has a lobby, auditorium and booth. Attend a performance through the theatre’s own schedule and ticket interactions.

  • Miyamoto staffs the theatre ticket counter.
  • The booth has manual lighting controls for eligible operators.
  • Homeless in Denton is among the recorded material shown on stage.
29 / The Card Game

Beaver Slap (447 cards)

Build a deck and fight for territory.

Beaver Slap has 447 cards, including 285 character cards, and uses a five-by-five board. Character, item and location cards have their own effects. A card’s direction matters when it clashes with a neighbour.

  • Start an exhibition from the main menu.
  • Read the card direction and abilities before placing it.
  • Browse the card collection separately from the Story roster.
30 / Departures

Beaver Valley Air

Check the ticket, the seat class and the destination.

Buy a fare and board through the gate. First-class and economy routes have different cabins and journeys. Economy tickets sold for Washington or Russia can lead to the war front.

  • The first-class cabin and cargo hold are separate onboard spaces.
  • Boarding and disembarkation follow the flight’s state.
  • Beaver Valley Air traffic also appears in Ocean Operations.
31 / Consequence

Your Death Is Real Too

Story casualties affect the world you are playing in.

A permanent Story death changes the available actors in that world. The game can return you to character selection for a new playable identity. Versus and other standalone activities have their own rules.

  • Surviving party members keep their own state.
  • A combat injury and a confirmed death have different effects.
  • The hospital can treat living casualties.
32 / The Bestiary

Things That Hunt You

Some encounters have teeth, wings or a heat signature.

The Beaver Eater, sewer snake, alligators, and armed drone are distinct encounters with their own movement and attack behavior. For example, the smaller alligator swims beneath UNSE.

  • Watch the creature rather than assuming it fights like a person.
  • Water and floor geometry affect where a creature can follow.
  • The drone has its own controls and targeting behavior.
33 / Wardrobe

Get Dressed

Preview wearables at Iron Mike’s.

Iron Mike’s catalogue lets you inspect clothing and equipment before buying. Wearables have their own slots and can change a character’s look.

  • Browse the catalogue from the shop counter.
  • The fitting preview displays the selected item.
  • Clothes in the bag and clothes being worn have separate states.
34 / The Agency

The A.S.S.

The federal detention complex has its own intake and custody.

American Security Services officers have named posts and duty appearances. The complex includes intake, holding rooms, a cellblock, a yard and support rooms.

  • Municipal custody is handled separately.
  • Gate and room access follow the native assignment or custody state.
  • Its personnel are grouped in the ASS Agents roster tab.
35 / Ocean Operations

Ocean Operations

Choose a mission, side, captain, and vessel.

Start from the mission library. Side, objectives and starting forces determine the craft available to command. The chart includes local shoals, deep water, ports and several connected operating regions.

  • The Last Ferry revolves around the Capybara passenger ferry.
  • Quiet Water offers submarine play.
  • The vessel selector rates the choices before launch.

36 / The Waterfront

Sail It Home

Walk ashore from supported docks and berths.

Approach an authored berth and complete the ship’s docking conditions to go ashore or enter a walkable room. The submarine pen connects to the world under the town.

  • A ship room and the ocean helm offer different views.
  • Civilian craft use their own docking checks.
  • Movement between regions follows the chart’s mapped routes.
37 / Command

Give the Order Out Loud

Shipboard voice control uses the selected station.

Voice commands can route orders to a supported helm or station, with spoken responses from available crew. Commands depend on the active vessel, selected target and authority.

  • Check your current station before issuing the order.
  • A contact may still need identification.
  • Fleet Command also accepts typed interface actions.
38 / Damage Control

She Floods One Room at a Time

Damage changes the systems you can still use.

Wounds, water, fire and crew assignments influence how a ship responds after a hit. Damage control can isolate a room and send crew when conditions allow it.

  • Check the affected compartment and nearby hatches.
  • Flooding and propulsion damage can change your practical speed.
  • The bridge and engineering rooms expose different controls.
39 / The Fleet

Pick a Vessel on Either Side

Mission, faction, and starting conditions determine your options.

Beaver Valley and the Russian side have distinct ships and equipment. Civilian boats, merchants, carrier aircraft and launched boats use separate ways of entering play.

  • The Fleet directory lists speed, hull, equipment, crew posts and passenger seats where the game provides them.
  • The ferry has six crew posts and a 22-knot rating.
  • Some craft appear as ambient traffic or as a ship’s embarked complement.
40 / The Deep

Something Lives Down There

Some deep-water contacts are neither ships nor aircraft.

Lotar is a distinct underwater encounter that can approach and attack surface traffic. The Beaver submarine and the Angler submersible have different operating roles.

  • The Angler is carried in an OX cradle.
  • Sonar and submerged views help find underwater contacts.
  • The encounter shown is Lotar surfacing around the Skunk tanker.
41 / Aviation

Everything That Comes Off the Deck

Helicopters, jets and boats have their own handovers.

Flight decks can host aircraft tied to their ship. Carrier wings take orders, and players can take a supported aircraft through the native takeover controls.

  • The Mara has a carrier fighter and a deck helicopter.
  • Escort helicopters support search and other assigned jobs.
  • Recovery depends on the deck and the aircraft’s approach.
42 / Working Boat

The Muskrat Fishing Boat

Find a catch and take it back to port.

The Muskrat carries fishing equipment and a hold. Use its fish finder, harpoon, and net aboard the boat, then return to a harbour that accepts the catch.

  • The fishing boat is a civilian vessel.
  • Keep track of the hold as you fish.
  • The guide lists its in-game ratings alongside other working boats.
43 / Consequence

The Town Is a Target on the Naval Chart

Shore locations can be part of the operation.

The naval chart can display shore targets and structures in connected operating regions. An incoming strike can affect the active world through the authored impact and destruction systems.

  • Check the current region and contact plot.
  • The Grand FOB strike shows a native destruction sequence.
  • Results depend on the weapon and target.
44 / Conversation

They Start It

A nearby resident can start a conversation.

Some eligible characters initiate speech when a player approaches. The game considers the speaker, what the player is doing, recent interactions and the current encounter.

  • Hold Talk to address someone yourself.
  • The intended person must be available to respond.
  • Recorded scenes and silent characters have their own speech rules.
45 / The Nuke

Strategic Ordnance

Some operations include a special targeting console.

The Beaver can carry a separately tracked nuclear round when the operation permits it. Its impact and interception are simulated in the current region.

  • The special round has its own targeting interface.
  • Use the chart to inspect the assigned area.
  • The stock position and loadouts depend on the operation.
46 / The Fleet Is Yours

Both Navies Have a Carrier

Control a ship or give orders from Fleet Command.

Fleet Command shows eligible friendly vessels, contacts, routes and per-unit controls. Carriers have an air-wing view for missions and aircraft availability.

  • The Beaver Valley carrier is Mara and the Russian carrier is Kodiak.
  • Available magazines and aircraft depend on the unit.
  • Orders can continue while you switch back to your own helm.
47 / Piracy

Take the Ship Instead of Sinking It

Crew a launch and prepare a boarding party.

Eligible ships deploy a rigid-hull boat with a helmsman and boarders. The boarding UI tracks team assignments, and once aboard the outcome depends on the defending crew and the operation.

  • The Weasel’s boat has four seats including its helm.
  • Boarding and ship transfer use distinct steps.
  • Crew posts can be reviewed aboard a controlled ship.
48 / Operations

Write the Battle, Then Fight It

Set up an operation in the mission editor.

Choose starting units and sides, give them positions or routes, and define objectives. The editor saves a custom operation you can launch from the library.

  • Briefings explain the starting situation.
  • Use the chart to place eligible craft.
  • Parameters determine the choices available on each side.
49 / The Strait

Somewhere That Actually Exists

The Straight of Booze uses real geographic data.

The fictional operation uses measured shoreline, elevation and depth data from the Strait of Hormuz narrows. The chart and 3D terrain share the same coastline.

  • The game fits a 70-kilometre section of the real narrows into a 50-kilometre playable map.
  • A shallow bank and a deep channel have different navigation consequences.
  • The other operating regions have their own maps.
