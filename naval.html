Ocean Operations

Naval Combat

Take the Weasel frigate out to meet the Capybara ferry. In The Last Ferry, Beaver Valley wins when the ferry reaches its rally point alive. Play the Russian side and that same crossing becomes an interception.

Choose an operation

Open Ocean Operations in the mode menu. Pick a briefing and one of its available sides, then choose your vessel and captain. The mission decides which ships and aircraft you can command.

The Last Ferry

The Capybara carries passengers through the Beaver Valley Approaches. Track the ferry on the chart while its Weasel frigate and Rabbit cutter cover the crossing.

Quiet Water

The Beaver and Otter submarines share the approaches with a surface escort apiece. Each side is looking for the other submarine. Losing an escort leaves the hunt open.

Eighteen-Minute Screen

The Mara carrier group faces the Kodiak and its escorts. Beaver Valley can win by keeping a mission vessel afloat for eighteen minutes of simulation time.

Command the fleet

Read the chart

Fleet Command shares the contacts detected by your side. An unidentified contact gets a track label. When your sensors identify it, the ship or aircraft gets a name. Losing the track can take away the option to attack.

Give each ship a job

Send a unit to a waypoint, put it on patrol, have it hold position, or assign it to escort another vessel. An attack order depends on the contact, the equipment aboard and the ammunition left. Supported helm and fleet orders also accept voice commands.

The fight at sea

Surface ships

The Weasel frigate has a main gun, torpedoes, missiles and close-in defence. Its weapons panel shows what remains aboard. Fleet Command can issue a single shot or give the ship an ongoing attack order.

Submarines

At the Beaver’s helm you can change depth, manage the torpedo tubes and release acoustic decoys. During Quiet Water, the Otter and its surface escort produce different contacts for the crew to sort out.

Defence and damage reports

Equipped units can engage incoming fire automatically and carry countermeasures suited to the craft. Identify an opposing hull to see a damage assessment of its engines, sensors and weapon systems.

Off the flight deck

Carrier aviation

The Mara and Kodiak operate fighters and helicopters. Configure a surviving, parked aircraft on the carrier and choose a mission. Fighters can fly air patrol or intercept a contact. A helicopter equipped for anti-submarine work can investigate the fight below the surface.

Board another ship

Open the RHIB crew panel on an equipped vessel. Choose the boarding party and its equipment, then launch the rigid-hulled inflatable boat. The coxswain stays with the boat while the party boards an eligible ship. Defenders can resist or surrender, and you can recall the party.

When the ship takes a hit

A ship can still be afloat and already have lost the equipment you counted on. An engine hit can reduce its top speed, damaged sensors can shorten its reach, and a damaged mount can take a weapon out of service.

Below decks

A breach can flood a compartment, and water can spread through an open connecting door. On ships with interiors, inspect the affected room. Damage-control sailors can collect extinguishers to put out fires and work together to patch a breach.

Ship’s company

The fleet has named crews. Helena Ward captains the Mara, with Everett Sloane as executive officer. The crew postings screen lets you assign available people to shipboard work, including engineering, damage control and medical duties.

Beyond the battle group

Working boats

The Muskrat fishing boat carries a net, a harpoon and a hold for the catch you bring back to sell. Merchant ships carry cargo between ports, wait for a berth and unload.

The coast and the deep

The submarine pen connects the ocean to a walkable dock in the sewers. Bring the Beaver into the pen to go ashore, or put back to sea from the water’s edge. The Beaver Valley chart also marks buildings along the waterfront. Farther offshore, Lotar, the abyssal serpent, can seize a tanker and drag it below the surface. The separate Straight of Booze chart uses shoreline and seabed data from the Strait of Hormuz.

Civilian flights

For a quieter trip through the same mode, select a civilian sightseeing mission. In Moscow, the unarmed Chipmunk helicopter starts in flight near the Moskva River, with the Kremlin and Red Square ahead.

Build your own mission

Choose Create New Mission in the mission library. Pick an operating area, place the participants, assign sides and draw their routes. Give each side its own goal, such as bringing a ship into an area or surviving for a set time, or leave it open-ended. Saved scenarios appear in your local mission library.

Vessels and aircraft

Explore 65 ships, small craft and aircraft. Compare top speeds, hull strength, crew posts and equipment. Mission sides and briefings determine the choices at the start. Other craft launch from a ship or a berth, or appear as traffic during play.

Speeds are fresh, undamaged game ratings. Hull points measure the live simulation’s damage scale. Crew posts count assigned jobs aboard. Passenger places are listed wherever the game gives a separate limit.

Shipping traffic

Survivors can share a four-seat life raft that appears as a rescue track. The naval bowling mission uses an unmanned target barge with ten resettable pins. Launchpad’s stern has a tender-garage door. Its two helipads host the yacht’s Chinchilla. The operations chart also tracks the player-built Beaver Machine on land. Its ground speed is 28 kn, its hull has 100 points and one operator can take control.
